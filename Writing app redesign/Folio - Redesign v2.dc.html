Part One · Chapter One {{ docMeta }}

The ferry did not come at six, and Sarah understood then that the winter had arrived in the way winters arrive here — not with snow but with cancellation, with the small municipal admissions that nothing further would be leaving tonight.

She waited anyway. There was ash on the sill of the terminal window, from the burn pits across the water, and she wrote her name in it with one finger and then wiped it out again.

Tomas had said the crossing takes forty minutes in good weather, and he had never once in eleven years described the weather as good. That was the whole of him, she thought: a man who kept his hopes in the subjunctive.

· · ·

By seven the ticket agent had gone home. The lights above the ramp stayed on, tuned to that particular yellow that makes everyone underneath it look like they have already left.

She counted the bollards. She counted them again and got a different number, which felt, in the circumstances, entirely fair.
